The range, and where this hour sits in it

No second line under an hour any more: a row is figures and nothing opens beneath it. The range glyph in the middle of the row is redrawn three ways, and tapping it opens the hour’s figures (each model, the gusts, the ensembles, your range). There’s no advice in it.

What the glyph draws today

Hour 12 below, drawn as RangeBar.tsx draws it, three times as large. The bar is a 0–30 knot scale (0–35 mph). Only 0, your minimum and your best are labelled in the column head. Nothing is labelled at 35, and the magenta box looks the same as the magenta tick.

The dot is the models’ consensus mean wind for the hour, a weighted median of the models (11 mph).
The grey whisker runs from the lowest model’s mean wind to the highest (9–11 mph). It’s drawn only when there’s more than one model.
The dotted pink tick is your minimum, below which you can’t stay on the foil for long (13 mph for this rider).
The pale pink box is your best wind, the “sweet spot” from windBand(rider) (17–29 mph).
The magenta bar is the gust, the median of the models’ gusts (30 mph).
A sea-blue bar, which wasn’t in your screenshot, is the ensembles’ middle 80% (10th–90th percentile). It’s drawn only when ensemble members reach the hour.
  1. The bar is 112 px wide but runs to 35 mph, and the right end has no tick and no label.
  2. Nothing in the row or the head says which mark is the forecast and which is you.
  3. The gust tick is the same magenta as your band, so it reads as part of the band.
  4. The column is hidden on phones (max-wide:hidden), so most readers never see it.

The lane: a labelled scale that runs down the column

The same linear scale, but treated as one chart down the column rather than a strip per row. Your range is a lane that runs unbroken from row to row, with your minimum and maximum as dashed edges. The scale ends at 40 with a labelled tick, so it has a visible start and end. Each mark gets its own shape: the forecast is ink (dot and capped whisker) and the gust is an orange caret, not magenta. Read down the column, the dots move in and out of the lane like a sparkline.

    B

    The chip: no scale, just where it sits

    The scale goes from the row entirely. A chip names where the models’ mean wind sits against your range: 4 under your minimum, light (between your minimum and your best), in range, strong (between your best and your maximum), or 3 over. It has a dashed edge when the models straddle a boundary, and a g↑ when the gusts go past your maximum. It fits a phone, where today’s glyph is hidden. The scale itself moves into the popover, where it has room for its labels.

      C

      The frame: the scale is your range

      A compact bullet: the box is your usable range, with its left edge at your minimum (13) and its right edge at your maximum (36). Your best is the tinted middle. Every row has the same box, so the start and end are its edges. The forecast’s dot and whisker sit inside it. A wind below or above it is pinned to a short stub outside the box with an arrow, so “under” and “over” read as shapes. The trade-off is that the stubs don’t show how far under. The popover does.

        The popover is the same for all three: tap or click any glyph (it’s a button; Enter or Space opens it, Escape or a tap elsewhere closes it, and focus goes back to the glyph). It holds the hour’s figures only: each model’s wind, gust and direction, the median and spread, how well the models agree, the ensembles’ 10–90%, the figures against your range, and the shore, but no session names and no coaching. Per-model figures need spotForecast(…, { perModel: true }), which the spot page doesn’t ask for yet.

        Figures are illustrative but consistent with the screenshot: an intermediate at about 95 kg, so minimum 11 kn (13 mph), best 15–25 kn (17–29 mph) and maximum 31 kn (36 mph), at a beach facing south.
