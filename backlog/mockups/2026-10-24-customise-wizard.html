The stars, gusts included: the plan

1. One top limit, and the gusts count against it

The gust step goes. The wind step keeps four handles (too light, the sweet spot's two ends, too much), and too much is compared with the gust, not the mean. "I can hold 30 knots" means 30 in the gusts, which is how riders say it.

  • Mean below the floor: no stars, as now. Gusts below the floor can still lift it to one star, as now.
  • Gust at or under your top: the mean alone decides the stars, as now.
  • Gust over your top: no stars, whatever the mean. It's too windy for you. The hour shows no stars at all (not outline ones), and its gust figure turns red.

2. Gusty or smooth: a setting, shown as outline stars

A separate question from "too strong": how uneven the wind is. We measure it as the gust over the mean (the gust factor), banded:

The rider says how much gusty wind bothers them: Not much, Some (default) or A lot. The stars it takes off are drawn as outline stars, the way every other lost star already is (solid = what you get, outline = what the wind would have been worth). So a gusty hour reads as, say, ★★★☆☆: the wind is good, but it is not smooth.

Watch out: the very first rating (v1) took stars off for any gust factor over 1.35, and Irish model winds nearly always exceed that. On 4 October Dublin Bay sat at zero all day while people were out riding (docs/STAR_RATINGS.md, "Why v2"). That's why "gusty" starts at 1.5, not 1.35. Before building, I'd replay a week of published hours to check how many lose a star.

3. The shore: a web you drag

Each spoke is a wind direction relative to the beach, pointing the way the wind blows: offshore points out to sea, onshore points in over the land. The dot on each spoke sets what that wind costs: at the rim it costs nothing, each ring in is one star off, and the centre is never. Left and right are one setting, so dragging one side moves its mirror.
