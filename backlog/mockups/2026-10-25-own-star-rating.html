How the stars come from four numbers

The rider never sees a gust setting. From the slider's four handles, with the gust read 5 kn lighter than it is (fixed, ours):

  • Steady wind: none below too light; one star at too light, climbing a star a knot to four just below the sweet spot; five through the sweet spot; then four for three knots, down to one star at too much − 5 and none from too much − 3.
  • Gusts: a gust past too much is no stars. Otherwise the gust is read 5 kn lighter on the same scale and the hour takes the lower of the two; the stars it costs are outlined.
  • Shore: what the web says, taken off after; offshore is always none.

Against the agreed table (ours: too light 11, sweet 15–22, too much 35)

Defaults chosen: 11 · 15–22 · 35. Too much at 35 puts one star at 30 and none from 32, as agreed; 36 or 37 would leave a star at 32–33. Too light at 11 matches 11–14 kn exactly. The only differences from the plan are at the very ends, in red: 10 kn steady (the plan's tail gave it a star; here, under too light, none) and a gust of exactly 36 (the plan gave one star; here a gust past too much is none).

For you to decide

  1. Should the upwind course move stars? Here it moves none. A rider who can't point upwind in a dead onshore wind can't get out; should that hour lose a star?
  2. The 10 kn hour. Too light at 11 gives 10 kn nothing. Is that right, or should the default be 10 (which also lifts 11–13 kn a star)?
  3. Whole stars on the web. The site's web moves in half stars; here it snaps to whole ones, as the stars are drawn whole. Keep half stars?
  4. About you. With the levels gone, step 1 is weight and units (the units are drawn but do nothing in this mock-up). "Wings you own" said a star comes off when none suits; wings never cost a star now, so it is left out. Drop it, or keep it for picking your own wing elsewhere?
  5. Downwind edge range. 0–45° below across, 10° by default. Right?
