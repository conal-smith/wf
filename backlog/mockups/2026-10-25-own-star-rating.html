Which one

A, the wind ruler. It is the only one where the rating is something you can see whole: one shape on a knots axis, the same axis the wing sizes sit on, with ours as a ghost so a change reads as the gap between two outlines rather than as marks on a hundred cells. The gust rule stops being a sentence and becomes the same shape slid right by N knots, which you can drag. On a phone the handles still drag, and the four steppers under it do the same job for a thumb, so A on a phone is C with the picture kept.

B is the friendliest first step but a poor editor: the fit is a black box, the rider can't see why tapping one card moves another, and some answers (15 g25 two stars, 20 g25 five) can't be fitted at all. Worth keeping as an optional "rate four hours" start that hands over to A. C is the plainest and the most thumb-friendly, but it hides the shape; it survives inside A as the phone's steppers.

The week under every editor is what makes any of them work: it is where a rider sees what their change means for their days. "Compare with ours" rings the hours that changed and nothing else; the every-combination grid stays folded away, and in compare mode outlines the region that changed instead of putting ▲▼ in each cell.

For you to decide

  1. Three handles or four? Four stars end 3 kn after five does (or 2 kn before the stars run out), as in the plan. A fourth handle for "four stars to" is easy to add to the ruler, but every handle is one more thing to explain.
  2. The light end. The climb to five starts 6 kn below "five from" and is tied to it. Should "stars start" be its own handle, for riders who pump in 10 kn?
  3. Weight moves the wing only. Here weight changes the wing sizes and never the stars. Agreed?
  4. Where the wing figure shows. Under the ruler and under each hour, as "4.5". Should the chart's dock and the spot cards carry it too, and does it need "m²" anywhere but this screen's label?
  5. Limits. Gusts read 0–10 kn lighter; cross-offshore 0–2 stars; five stars from 10–25 kn; the stars run out at least 4 kn after five ends, at 40 kn at most. Right ranges?
  6. Units. Drawn in knots. A rider on mph or Beaufort would see the ruler in their unit (as the wizard's wind step does today).
  7. Where it lives. As a step in Customise in place of "What wind suits you?" and its gusty setting, or as its own screen reached from Customise and from the stars' "why" box?
