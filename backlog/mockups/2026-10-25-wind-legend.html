What does this green mean?

The tidal depth and the currents have keys. The coloured wind has a key only inside the Layers panel and on About the map, so on the chart itself the colours go unexplained. Here are three ways to keep the key on the chart while Coloured wind is on. Move the pointer over the sea: the key points at the wind under it.

The three

How each one works

1 · Strip

A thin scale above the timeline, 0 to 40 knots. It sits where the eye drops from the chart to the hours, and it reads left to right like the wind getting up.

It takes a little of the sea at the foot of the chart.

2 · Column

A short upright scale under the zoom and Layers buttons. It takes the least room and stays out of the way of the spots.

Its numbers are small, and it's narrow on a phone.

3 · Steps

Six named bands rather than a ramp: the easiest to read at a glance, and it says in words what each colour is good for.

The coloured wind itself is smooth, so the bands are a simplification.

Before we build it

Questions for you

  1. Which of the three? My pick is 1 · Strip with your range bracketed. It's clearest on both desktop and phone.
  2. Your range on the key? The bracket shows the winds your star rating likes (from Customise; 15–25 kn here, as in the rating plan). It ties the colours to the stars. It would follow whatever we settle in the rating plan.
  3. The wind under the pointer? On a desktop the key points at the wind under the mouse and a tag shows the knots. On a phone, a tap on the sea could do the same.
  4. When does it show? The proposal: only while Coloured wind is on, and not for Particles alone (particles show direction and movement, not a scale). It could also fold away with a tap.
  5. The top of the scale. The key in the Layers panel runs to 60 kn. On the chart, 0–40 kn gives the rideable winds more room. Stronger winds still show, at the end.
