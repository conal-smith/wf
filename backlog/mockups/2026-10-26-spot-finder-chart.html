    The Spot finder, with the chart beside it

    The Spot finder becomes the home page. The cards are the ones on /spots today, unchanged. On a wide screen the real chart sits on the right, the full height of the page under the header, zoomed to the spots on the list. The expand button opens the chart full screen, and that is how you get into the chart. On a phone you see the list, with a black Map button at the bottom to switch between the list and the map.

    What to try

    • Hover a card. Its spot on the map turns black and shows its name, and the map moves to it. Move off the list and the map goes back to show all the spots.
    • Hover a spot on the map. Its card is outlined in the list. Click a spot on the map beside the list to scroll the list to its card. The map beside the list never opens the forecast dock.
    • Switch between Favourites and Near you. The map shows only the spots on the list, and zooms to fit them. The blue dot is you.
    • Press Full chart (top left of the map). The map grows to fill the screen and becomes the chart, with its own search box, controls and every spot. Press the black List button, the collapse button (top right), Escape or the browser's Back button to return to the list.
    • On the full chart, click a spot. The chart's forecast dock opens along the bottom, as it does on the chart today, and the spot gets the chart's red pen loop and range ring. There is no card over the map: to see the card, go back to the list.
    • With the dock open, the black List button moves up to sit just above it, and moves back down when the dock closes (the × on the dock, or Escape). Press the arrow on the dock for more rows; the button moves with it. On a phone the dock is the chart's bottom sheet, and the List button sits above it.
    • Click an hour in the dock to pick it. The stars on the map follow.
    • Press a card's picture to open the full chart at that spot, with its dock open.
    • Pick an hour on any card. Every card and every spot on the map shows the stars for that hour.
    • Drag and scroll the map to move and zoom it.
    • Change the width with the buttons in the black bar, or by resizing the window. From 1100 pixels wide the map sits beside the list. Below that the map is hidden and the black Map button appears, as on Airbnb. Under 720 pixels it is the phone layout.

    What is real and what is not

    • The map is two screenshots of the real chart running on this branch (Ireland, and the Dublin coast in more detail), placed with the same map maths the chart uses. The spots and the blue dot are drawn on top so they can respond to the mouse. They are drawn the way the chart draws its spots.
    • Only the Dublin coast has the detailed picture, so zooming in elsewhere looks soft. In the real build it is the live chart, sharp at every zoom.
    • The forecast dock is rebuilt in HTML from the real one (src/ForecastDock.tsx and src/dock.css, checked against screenshots of the running app at 1440 and 390), with the same made-up forecast as the cards. On a phone, tap the grip or the arrow to raise the sheet instead of dragging it.
    • The cards are drawn the way src/finder/SpotCard.tsx draws them, with a made-up forecast. The real Near you list has every spot within 30 km (23 from this location). The mock-up shows the 12 nearest so the file stays small.

    How it would be built

    • One chart, moved, not two. The page that draws the chart (src/App.tsx) would draw the finder and one WindMap together. The map sits in a box that is either the panel on the right or the whole screen. Expanding changes the box's size and calls map.resize() once at the end of the animation. The map is never unmounted, so it keeps its style, tiles, forecast frames and wind particles, and the browser keeps one WebGL context instead of two.
    • Panel and full chart differ only in what the map is given. In the panel: only the listed spots as lights, no loupe or dock, the hour picked on the cards, and the map fitted to the list. Full screen: every spot, the dock, the search box and the layers, exactly as the chart is today. The finder already gets the chart's rated hours from App, so nothing new has to be loaded.
    • The dock stays as it is. It only appears when the chart is full screen, never in the side panel. The List button would sit inside the dock's own layout, as the dock's tools and the chart's small print already do, so it moves with the dock's top edge in the same frame and can never cover it.
    • Hover. A card passes its spot's id to the lights (a class toggled in force5/src/lights.ts, no React re-render) and calls easeTo on the map after a short delay, so scrolling past cards does not throw the map around.
    • Addresses. / becomes the finder. The full chart is the same page with ?map in the address (pushed to history, so Back returns to the list). Existing links keep working: /?spot=keel opens the full chart on Keel, and /spots redirects to /. The "open the page you used last" memory in src/lastView.ts could then remember whether the chart was open.
    • Phone. The map is not created until the Map button is first pressed, as the old finder did. After that it is kept, hidden, and resized when shown again.

    Risks

    • Speed on a laptop. A WebGL map with moving wind particles next to a long list of SVG-heavy cards. Scrolling the list while the particles animate could stutter on older machines. Ways round it: fewer particles in the panel, pause them while the list scrolls, and only draw the cards near the screen.
    • The expand animation. Resizing a WebGL canvas every frame is costly. Better to animate the box with a transform or clip and resize the map once at the end.
    • Phone memory. The map, its forecast frames and 20 or more cards with pictures in one page. iOS Safari reloads tabs that use too much memory. Creating the map only when asked, and dropping it after a long time hidden, keeps that down.
    • Hover moving the map can feel busy. The delay, and returning to the whole list when the mouse leaves, should be tried with real use. It is easy to switch to "light up only, do not move".
    • The bottom of the full chart is busy: the dock, its tools on the right, the small print on the left and the List button in the middle. Where the window is too narrow for the button between them, the mock-up lifts the button above the dock's tools. The real build should check this at every width.
    • The chart's first load gets heavier for people who only wanted the list. The panel should wait until the cards are drawn before it asks for the map.
