What changed, and why

Surfline's map page has a narrow list beside a plain map, and a "Charts" switch on the map that turns it into a forecast chart with wind, waves, particles or arrows, and a time bar. This applies that to our Spot finder. Today the list takes most of the screen and the map beside it already draws the particles. Here the map is plain until the rider asks for the chart.

  1. 1A narrow list, 400 px. Each card is the spot's real satellite picture, the name and distance, three figures (stars, wind and gust with direction, water at the launch) and a strip of today's hours coloured by stars, with the best hours written under it. That's the Surfline card with our figures. The five-day row on today's card goes to the spot page, one tap away. Hovering a card lights its pin; hovering a pin lights its card.
  2. 2A plain map, then the Chart switch. At first the map has every layer off: land, sea, place names, and the listed spots as pills showing the wind, filled in the star colour. No particles, wash, soundings or tints. Chart (bottom, in magenta) turns the same map into the chart: the layer tabs, the settings, every spot as the chart's own lights and the time bar. Map turns it back. Full chart still opens today's full-screen chart, with the dock.
  3. 3Live wind on the plain map. Surfline's map has "Wind stations" and "Wave buoys" beside "Charts". Ours is Live wind: the latest readings from the buoys and Met Éireann stations our wind-obs job already reads every hour (plus Irish Lights' buoys, if we get their licence). The readings here are made up.
  4. 4Settings, as Surfline's. Which way it blows: Particles (the chart's today), Arrows, or None. Then wind in colour on or off, and the spots and live wind on or off. The tabs (Wind, Rain, Currents, Foil depth, Sea chart) are the layers the chart already has; this only puts them one tap from the map.
  5. 5The time bar. Drag, step or play through the week. The pins, the lights, the list's figures and the particles follow the hour. The model is named for the hour (Harmonie, then ECMWF), as on the spot page. In this mock-up the coloured wind is one photograph of one hour, so it doesn't change; in the real build it is the chart's own layer and moves with the hour.

Round two (owner's steer, 31 October)

  • Modern white and dark in place of the paper, with the moon button in the header; "Paper (today)" stays in the mock-up's bar for comparison. The map pictures are recoloured to match: in white, light grey land and a pale blue sea; in dark, near-black sea and dark grey land, with the wind's colours dimmed. That's a stand-in done on the photographs. The real build needs a white and a dark MapLibre style.
  • Enhanced satellite pictures on the cards: dehazed, a little more colour, then local contrast and a fine sharpen. "As today" in the bar shows the originals.

What is real and what is not

  • Real: the map pictures (wingfoil.ie's chart on 31 October, the camera set exactly in MapLibre), the 16 spot pictures, and the spots' places and facings.
  • Rain, currents and foil depth were photographed for the Dublin coast only. Zoomed out to Ireland, those tabs say so. In the real build they're live everywhere, and foil depth from zoom 9 as today.
  • Made up: the forecast, the stars, the depths and the live readings.

How it would be built

  • One WindMap, as the approved finder already has. "Plain" is the same map with wind: {wash: false, particles: false} and every layer in LAYER_DEFAULTS off but the names; the panel never reads the forecast frames until Chart is pressed, so the home page loads faster.
  • The pills are a second look for force5/src/lights.ts: the same DOM buttons, styled by a class on the map.
  • Arrows: a grid layer drawn from the same wind frame as the particles.
  • The rider's choice (plain or chart, which tab, which direction) is kept in the store, as the chart's layers are today.
