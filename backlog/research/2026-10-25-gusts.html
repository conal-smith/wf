Gusts and the stars: what should a gust cost?

What riders, coaches, forecasters and the weather itself say about gusts, measured against this site's own forecasts. Seven ways to score them, each run over every mean and gust for all three levels.

Rating code read at main 183926a (force5/src/stars.ts); nothing in it was changed. Riding level shown as the site labels it: Learning, Riding, Advanced (80 kg).

What we found

  1. The forecasts' gusts at the spots are much bigger than gusts at sea. Out at sea, the Irish weather buoys record gusts about 1.3× the mean wind at any speed. The models agree with them there. At the 149 riding spots, the same models give 1.6–1.9×, because a coastal grid point is treated partly as land. That puts the gust where the WMO table for overland wind puts it.
  2. Today's "gusty" (1.5×) is the normal state of a forecast hour, not a warning. At the spots it docked a star from 67% of Riding hours across September and October (HARMONIE). Gusts over the rider's limit wiped out another 29%. Only 5% were left alone. In this week's live run, 77% of hours at or above the floor have no stars because of the gust limit. For Learning it is 82–98%.
  3. The forecast cannot tell gusty hours from steady ones. We set model gusts against three Met Éireann stations hour by hour (Sep 2025 to Mar 2026). The model's gust factor barely predicts the observed one (Spearman 0.02–0.22). The spread, gust minus mean, only predicts it as far as the mean already does. The gust value is predicted well (0.74–0.84), as well as the mean is. So "how strong is the gust?" can be trusted, but "how gusty is the hour?" largely cannot.
  4. Riders judge gusts in knots, against their own range. Coaches and forums agree on three things. Gusts get you onto the foil in light wind. Lulls drop you off it. You choose a wing for the gusts. The rules of thumb come in knots: under 5 kn of spread is clean, about 8 kn is a beginner's limit, and 10 kn or more is "a rodeo". The ratio rules disagree wildly, from 1.2× to 2×. No school or association publishes a limit. Most of this is opinion.
  5. Your three examples already pass today's rule on paper. 20 g30 loses a star for being gusty, 25 g30 loses nothing and 10 g25 keeps its one star. But both 20 g30 and 25 g30 end up with 4★, and the cliff at the floor is real (10 g18 earns 1★, 11 g20 earns 0★). Only an allowance that grows with the mean (option E) both ranks 25 g30 above 20 g30 and keeps 10 g25. Applied to the forecast's own numbers, though, it docks over half of all riding hours.
Recommendation

Judge the gust you'd feel, by the rules you already ride by (option G), in two steps

  1. Now: phase the gusty cost in between the floor and the sweet spot (option B). This is a one-line change. It removes the cliff (no more "a knot more wind, a star less") and nothing else moves.
  2. Next: turn the forecast's gust into a felt gust, then apply E's rules to it. To get the felt gust, take three-quarters of the forecast's spread over the mean. That is what the stations show, and the shore angle can refine it later. E's rules then mean an allowance of max(6 kn, 0.4 × mean), which grows with the wind. Gust beyond the allowance costs up to 2 stars. The cost is larger the nearer the gust comes to your limit, and it phases in above the floor. Use the same felt gust for the "over your limit" test. Below the floor, keep today's lift. Keep the one existing setting, "how much gusty wind bothers you", as a ×0.5 / ×1 / ×1.5 multiplier.

Why G: it keeps your intuition, in knots and growing with the wind, and it uses the forecast's numbers only for what they are good at. It docks 40% of Riding hours instead of 67%, and zeroes 17% instead of 29% (Sep–Oct). It is monotone, has no cliff, and can be explained in one sentence.

Decisions for you
  1. Are your examples felt or forecast numbers? If "20 gusting 30" is what you'd feel on the water, take G. If it is what the forecast prints, take E: it matches your examples exactly but docks most riding hours at Irish spots.
  2. Should the "gusts over your limit" rule read the felt gust? G does, and that changes the ruling of 24 October in practice, though not in words. Learning is hit hardest: today 82% of its riding hours have no stars at all because of the gust limit. With the felt gust that is still 59%, which suggests Learning's 24 kn limit is itself tight for Irish winds.
  3. Configurable or one standard? Recommended: keep the one three-choice setting and expose no thresholds (see Configurable?). The level already moves where the allowance bites, through the rider's band.

1 · What gusts do to a wing foiler

  • Opinion In light wind, gusts are how you get up. SURF Magazin's coaching piece: "It's only during the gusts that you'll have enough lift to get airborne; in the lulls, you need to persevere."1 Duotone's Wing Academy: "As soon as you feel a gust hitting your wing, that's your cue to start pumping."2 This backs today's lift below the floor.
  • Opinion Lulls drop you off the foil, and gusts overpower. WindUp: "On the foil you ride through lulls easily, but a sudden drop pulls you off the foil", and "If gusts are running 8-10 knots over the mean, expect a stop-start session".3 Parawingfoil: "the peaks are what overwhelm you, not the mean".4
  • Opinion Sailors handle it the same way. A dinghy coach's order for a gust is "Ease, hike, trim", and "The strategy when sailing into a lull is to coast."15 That is the wing rider's flag-out and glide.
  • Opinion Steady beats strong. FoilPlanet: "consistent 15-knot winds provide better riding conditions than gusty 20-knot winds".5
  • Opinion A wing dumps power easily, which a kite cannot. On Seabreeze, riders describe letting go with the back hand or flagging the wing in a gust. One says a day of "low average wind and strong gusts" calls for a bigger board or foil and a smaller wing (search excerpt, unverified).6 This is why a wing forgives an occasional big gust more than a kite does.
  • Opinion Choosing a wing on a gusty day. The guides say go to the small end of your range: "If your spot is gusty rather than steady, lean toward the smaller end of your range so the gusts stay manageable."4 WindUp says "size your wing for the gusts, not the lulls".3 The old windsurfing saying is to rig the sail for the gusts and the board for the lulls (Seabreeze, unverified excerpt)8.
  • Opinion Beginners tolerate much less. Kite forums put the beginner's limit at 5–8 kn of spread7. The KiteReady app's makers say "The tolerance for gusts shifts" with level32. WindUp: "gusty 12–25 kt is far harder to learn in than a constant 16 kt."49

No formal limit exists. No search turned up a published maximum gust spread or factor from IKO, BKSA, RYA or VDWS. Schools decide case by case, for example "gusts deemed too strong" (Cascade Kiteboarding20). Two injury studies name gusts as a cause in kite accidents, but neither measures a threshold21. The knot rules cluster around 5 kn (clean), 8 kn (a beginner's limit) and 10 kn (hard for anyone). The ratio rules run from 1.2× to 2× and agree on nothing.

Nobody we could find scores gusts with a published formula. The established services either leave gusts out of their stars (Windguru) or simply show them (Windy, Windfinder). The newer apps state a principle without numbers (KiteReady), or use a hard cap (gust). A felt-gust allowance would be more careful than anything published, so it has to be explainable.

4 · The weather: what a forecast gust is

  • Measured A gust is the strongest 3-second wind. Under the WMO convention the forecast gust is the hour's peak 3-second wind at 10 m40. ECMWF's 10fg is the "10 metre wind gust since previous post-processing", a maximum35. HARMONIE's, as this site ingests it, is the maximum over the hour ending at the forecast time (docs/GUST_DATA.md). The mean wind, by contrast, is an instantaneous model value3138. So a forecast's "20 g30" means 20 on average, with one 3-second peak of 30 at some point in the hour. It does not mean 30 again and again.
  • Measured The surface sets the gust factor. The WMO's guidance gives the hour's 3-second gust over the mean as 1.30 at sea, 1.45 at a coast with onshore wind, 1.60 with offshore wind and 1.75 inland. Turbulence intensity runs 0.10, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.25 for the same four39. The buoys above sit at 1.31–1.33, right on the at-sea value.
  • Measured Light wind has a naturally high ratio. The gust factor falls as the wind rises: in one Chinese coastal study, from 3.6 at 0–2 m/s to 1.23 at 22–24 m/s41. A US Weather Service climatology found 1.94 over land below 10 kn and 1.38 at 20–25 kn17. That is why a fixed ratio flags light days first.
  • Measured Showers and unstable air add gusts. ECMWF adds a convective gust term only where deep convection is active, worth about 5–8 m/s near showers37. Its main term is 7.71 × friction velocity, which grows with surface roughness36. As a rough guide, gusts run 20–30% over the mean in stable air and 40–60% in unstable air16.
  • Measured What the forecast's spread implies for the lulls. The gust factor equals 1 + g × turbulence intensity, and with the WMO's 1.30 and 0.10 the hour's peak sits about 3 standard deviations over the mean39. A lull you meet again and again, about one standard deviation under, is therefore roughly 0.4 × the spread below the mean. Option D uses this.

Configurable?One setting, or one standard

The case for a setting. Tolerance genuinely differs. Forum limits run from 5 kn of spread to 15. KiteReady shifts gust tolerance by skill32. And wing riders who flag the wing out ride gusts that kiters would sit out6. The setting already exists and is stored, so keeping it costs nothing.

The case against exposing more. The forecast cannot say which hour will be the gusty one (data section). Finer control would only tune the noise, and the stars become harder to argue with. Thresholds in knots, a slider for the allowance, or a separate gust limit would all ask riders a question the forecast cannot answer.

Recommended: keep the three choices ("Not much", "Some", "A lot"). In G they scale the gust cost by ×0.5, ×1 and ×1.5, and change nothing else. The level already does the rest: the allowance bites harder for Learning because their limit is lower. No new control is needed. If you'd rather have one standard, G with "some" is that standard.

Not recommended: a per-rider allowance in knots. It is the one number riders would argue about, and the forecast's spread does not carry the information to make it mean anything hour by hour.
