How many stars should wind and gusts get?

A plan for you to decide. Nothing in the app has changed. Every star on this page is for a Riding (intermediate) rider of 80 kg, with no shore angle.

The recommendation

  • Five stars from 15 to 23 kn of mean wind. Four at 25, three at 28, two at 30, none from 35. More wind is not better.
  • Judge the gust by how strong it is, not by gust ÷ mean. Take 5 kn off the gust and rate it on the same scale as the mean. If that gives fewer stars, the hour gets that lower number. So 15 g25 and 20 g25 get five stars, and 20 g30 gets four.
  • Outline stars, one per reason. The solid stars are what the hour is worth for you. Each outline is a star lost to one named thing: gusts, cross-offshore or your wings. Tap or hover to see which.
  • Drop the "how much does gusty wind bother you" setting. Everyone gets one rule, and your level and weight still move it.

What the two numbers are. Wind is the model's mean wind 10 m above the surface, the same measure as a weather station's 10-minute average (your "persistent wind"). Gust is the strongest few-second gust the model expects at 10 m in the hour up to that time.

What the research says

  • Sweet spot

    About 14–20 kn for most intermediates, and up to 25 kn as they improve. No source calls 15–25 "the" range, but it's the band they all sit inside.WindUp, MACkite, Nootica (14–20 / 16–20 / 15–20); FoilPlanet (intermediates 15–25).

  • 25 kn

    Where it starts to get hard. "Most recreational riders start struggling above 25–28 kt." A 5 m wing tops out at 25 kn on the brands' charts.WindUp; F-One Strike V6 chart (5 m: 14–25 kn).

  • 30 kn

    Rideable, but a limit, not a good day. A sailing club stops all sailing when it is "consistently gusting over 30 knots". A school's wingfoil window ends at 30. A guide says 30+ "demand[s] specialized gear". A forum rider calls 30–40 "still totally possible to control but not much fun". The brands still sell 3 m wings for 28–38 kn.Chew Valley Lake SC; Northern Kites; FoilPlanet; Seabreeze (excerpt); F-One.

  • Gusts

    Riders talk in knots and about the peak. "15 to 20 with the sporadic gust to 25" is "more manageable than … gusts up to 30". Size your wing for the gust. 8–10 kn over the mean means a stop-start session. The rules based on gust ÷ mean range from 1.2× to 2×, so there's no agreed ratio.MACkite (kite coaching); WindUp; PR #202's survey of rules of thumb.

  • Our data

    Forecast gusts at our spots run high. At sea a forecast gust is about 1.3× the mean. At the spots it's 1.6–1.9×, so 15 g25 is an ordinary forecast hour. Against the stations, the model gets the gust's strength right but barely predicts gust ÷ mean.Measured in PR #202: Irish buoys, Met Éireann stations, our own blend.

Gap or ratio? Riders talk about the gap (under 5 kn is clean, 10+ is "a rodeo"), not the ratio. But neither can be trusted in a forecast. The gust's own strength can. That's why the plan judges the gust on its own. Is 15 g25 a five? If you'd feel a 10 kn gap on the water, the guides would lean to four. As a forecast at an Irish spot it's usually a smaller real gap, and both numbers are in range, so five is fair.

What an outline star means

The solid stars are what this hour is worth for you. Each outline is one star lost to one named thing: the gusts, the shore angle (cross-offshore, offshore) or your wings being the wrong size. So solid plus outline is what the wind alone would give. Every outline has a reason you can tap or hover to see. Two things don't use outlines. An orange or red warning replaces the stars outright. If gusts alone take a whole hour to zero, it shows none, as ruled on 24 October.

Dropping the gust setting

  • Customise loses its "Gusty wind: How much does uneven wind bother you? Not much · Some · A lot" row. Today it decides what a gust ≥ 1.5× the mean costs (0, 1 or 2 stars) and one ≥ 1.8× (1, 2 or 3).
  • Riders on "Not much" or "A lot" move to the one rule. A rider who wants fewer stars in strong wind can lower their own rating's top.
  • Saved settings, alerts and the MCP and companion tools keep accepting the old value for a while and ignore it, so nothing breaks. The Stars pages (web and app) lose their gust-setting table.

For you to decide

  1. 25 g30: four, or three plus an outline? The 5 kn allowance gives four. Any allowance small enough to make it three also drops 20 g30 to three. Only 4–5 kn fits all your examples.
  2. Where gusts end an hour. Proposed for Riding: a 40 kn gust is zero (today it's anything over 30). Or keep a hard line at 35.
  3. The other levels. Suggested: Learning gets five at 14–18 and none by 28. Advanced gets five at 15–26 and none by 38. Today Advanced gets five up to 26 kn and three at 30.
  4. Cross-offshore costs one whole star for Riding (today 1.5). Offshore stays as heavy as it is.
