The chart, four ways

Four takes on A, the Admiralty chart crossed with a broadsheet. In all four the map is the page: big, and zoomable from all of Ireland down to one beach. It shows the real coast, real depths and the real wind as moving ink lines, and the red pen still circles things and writes in the margin.

The depths are EMODnet's survey (which includes INFOMAR): about 250 m detail round the whole coast, and 115 m in the five featured bays. Tides are the Marine Institute's predictions; the forecast and stars are the real ones for 5–9 October. no made-up numbers this time

1

The Chart

Today's app, redrawn as an Admiralty chart. The closest to something we could ship.

Map
Full-screen chart. Spots are lights: a ring with the day's stars, and from three stars up a magenta flare flashing once per star, Fl(4)Mg. A title cartouche carries the scale, datum and key.
Time
The scrubber is set like a tide table, one block per day with its high and low waters. Drag it and the wind lines and every light move with it.
Dock
The selected spot hour by hour, set in type: stars, wind, wing, sessions and the tide curve. The pen loops the best window and writes the plan in the margin.
Spot page
A sheet over the chart with a generated headline, the week, the hours, and a depth profile out from the beach showing where a mast touches at low water.

2

Chart & Broadsheet

Keeps the newspaper and makes the chart the page. The answer to "but then we lose the paper".

Layout
The chart takes about 70% of the screen. A narrow column, The Wingfoil Almanac, runs down one side with one story per day and headlines written from the data ("Brandon goes four stars on Tuesday evening").
The trick
Reading moves the chart. Scroll or hover a paragraph and the chart flies to the places it names. The pen then annotates in order, like someone writing: circle, underline, wind arrow, the window, and the shallows. Click a spot on the chart and the paper scrolls to its story.
Depth
Water under 1.2 m at the story's hour (charted depth plus the tide) is hatched in red, and the pen says so.
Phone
The paper becomes the page, with the chart as a sticky band that follows the story you're reading.

3

Under the Keel

The bathymetry as a tool: is there enough water under my foil, and when?

Setting
Your mast length sits with weight and level; the water you need is the mast plus 45 cm. Weight and level re-rate the stars with the product's own v2 rating.
Tide
Drag through the week and the bay re-shades live from the survey grid. It cross-hatches red where your foil touches, single hatch where there's no margin, and stipples drying ground. Hover anywhere to read the water there.
Go
A strip of hours that are three stars or better and deep enough, circled in pen. This week runs into spring tides, so Friday's low water cuts five-star days in two.
Spot page
Tide against wind across the week, and a cross-section of the seabed out from the launch with your mast drawn to scale.
OpenSpot page
4

Ireland in Relief

B's 3D timeline, made of paper and standing on the bathymetry.

Seabed
Every depth band is a sheet of card stepping down from the coast, with ink edges, like a museum relief model. Ireland sits in a tray, and the vertical exaggeration is stated in the key.
Ribbon
The selected spot's five days of wind rise out of its bay. Height is wind, twist is direction, the tint deepens to magenta with the stars, a pink skin shows the gusts, and nights are hatched.
Time
Scrub or "Run the clock": a pin runs along the ribbon and the ink wind lines move below it. The pen circles the best window on the ribbon.
Camera
A slow fly-in from Ireland to the best bay. Drag to orbit, and one click gives the flat chart.

Mixing and matching

These are directions, not products, and the parts move between them. A sensible build would be:

  • 1 as the base (it is the app we have);
  • 2's reading-moves-the-chart for the "today" view, and its headlines;
  • 3's depth-under-the-foil as a layer and a spot page section, since nobody else gives riders that;
  • 4 as the showpiece spot view on desktop.

The depths, wind and tides come from scripts in tools/; the shared map style, wind lines and pen are in chart-kit.js; the brief the four were built to is BRIEF.md.
